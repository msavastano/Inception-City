# Assets

Almost everything in Inception City is made by code from a seed: the buildings, the streets, the sky, the drone and the music-box, and the bodies and clothes of its people. This file lists the few things that are not, where they came from, and the licence they came under.

**The rule:** anything that is not generated here must be CC0 (public domain dedication) and must be listed in this file before it is merged. No likenesses of real people, no brand marks, no film music.

## Animation: Universal Animation Library (Standard), by Quaternius

| | |
| --- | --- |
| What | How people move: the skeleton and eleven motion clips |
| Author | [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com) |
| Licence | [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) (the pack's own `License.txt`) |
| Got from | `https://opengameart.org/sites/default/files/universal_animation_librarystandard.zip`, file `Godot/AnimationLibrary_Godot_Standard.glb` |
| In the repository as | [`src/people/rigdata.ts`](src/people/rigdata.ts), generated by [`scripts/pack-people.mjs`](scripts/pack-people.mjs) |

What was taken from the pack:

- **22 of its bones** (the body without fingers), with their rest pose.
- **Eleven clips**, resampled and quantised to 16 bits: `Idle_Loop`, `Idle_Talking_Loop`, `Walk_Loop`, `Walk_Formal_Loop`, `Jog_Fwd_Loop`, `Sprint_Loop`, `Jump_Loop`, `Jump_Land`, `Hit_Chest`, `PickUp_Table` and `Punch_Jab`.

What was not taken: the pack's mannequin mesh, its textures, and its other clips. The bodies in the game are built in code ([`src/people/body.ts`](src/people/body.ts)) on the pack's skeleton, so the pack itself (14 MB) is not in the repository and nothing is downloaded when the game runs. The packed data is about 60 kB.

To regenerate `src/people/rigdata.ts` (for instance to add a clip: edit the list at the top of the script):

```bash
node scripts/pack-people.mjs                 # downloads the pack into a cache folder
node scripts/pack-people.mjs path/to/pack.zip   # or use a copy you already have
```

The script also measures each walking clip: how fast it was animated to move, how far one cycle carries the walker, and where in the cycle the left foot lands. The game moves each clip on by the ground its walker covers, which is why feet do not slide.

## Sound: recordings from OpenGameArt

The drone, the kick's BRAAAM, the wind and the heist's music box are made in code. The air of each level, the crowd's murmur and the footsteps are recordings. All of them were published on [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org) under [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) by the people below, and all were cut, filtered, levelled and re-encoded here, so no file is in the repository as its author uploaded it.

They are in the repository as the eight Ogg Opus files in [`src/fx/sounds/`](src/fx/sounds) (about 830 kB together, mono), generated by [`scripts/pack-sounds.mjs`](scripts/pack-sounds.mjs). The download addresses below all start with `https://opengameart.org/sites/default/files/`.

Nothing here is music, and nothing is from a film. The murmur has no words left in it and no voice that could be picked out.

The recordings were chosen from their authors' descriptions and from pictures and measurements of the sound (spectrograms, loudness), not by ear. If one sounds wrong, change its row in the tables at the top of the script and run it again:

```bash
node scripts/pack-sounds.mjs                 # downloads into a cache folder, then writes src/fx/sounds/
node scripts/pack-sounds.mjs --only room     # rebuild one file and keep the rest
```

The script needs `ffmpeg` (with libopus) and, for the two `.7z` packs, one of `7z`, `bsdtar` or Python's `py7zr`. Each looping file holds its loop followed by a short tail; the game fades the tail back in over the loop's start after decoding, so the seam cannot click. `steps.opus` holds every footstep end to end, and `manifest.ts` says where each one is. `tests/sound.test.ts` fails if the files grow past 1.5 MB or if a download in the script is missing from this page.

A browser that cannot decode Ogg Opus (Safari before 18.4) plays the game with its code-made sound only.

## Surface detail: scanned textures from Poly Haven

The colour of every street, pavement and wall is still painted by the game's shaders. What comes from outside is the *shape* of seven surfaces, close to: the grain of the asphalt, the aggregate in the pavements, the setts in the gutters and courtyards, the fans of setts round the monument and in the squares, the gravel on the park paths, the tooled face of the limestone, and the board marks in the concrete. Each is a photoscanned texture published on [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com) under [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); only its normal map, roughness and ambient occlusion were used, at half their 1k size, and none of its colour.

They are in the repository as the seven WebP files in [`src/city/detail/`](src/city/detail) (about 1.2 MB together) with their `manifest.ts`, generated by [`scripts/pack-detail.mjs`](scripts/pack-detail.mjs). Each file holds four grey 512 x 512 tiles: the surface's lean along each of its two axes, its roughness (as a change from its own average) and its cavities.

| Layer | Used for | Texture | Author | Tile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `asphalt` | the roadways and the Circus's ring road | [Asphalt 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/asphalt_01) | Dario Barresi and Charlotte Baglioni | 2.08 m |
| `aggregate` | the pavements, and (0.8 m a tile) the faces of the old-town bricks | [Gravel Embedded Concrete](https://polyhaven.com/a/gravel_embedded_concrete) | Charlotte Baglioni | 2 m |
| `setts` | a gutter of setts along every kerb, and the courtyards inside the blocks | [Cobblestone Floor 08](https://polyhaven.com/a/cobblestone_floor_08) | Rob Tuytel | 2 m |
| `fan` | setts laid in fans round the monument and in the middle of the squares | [Floor Pattern 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/floor_pattern_02) | Rob Tuytel | 1.2 m |
| `gravel` | the paths in the parks | [Rocky Trail](https://polyhaven.com/a/rocky_trail) | Amal Kumar | 2 m |
| `stone` | the limestone walls and window surrounds, the kerbs, flagstones and concrete yards | [Concrete Floor 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/concrete_floor_02) | Rob Tuytel | 2 m |
| `boards` | the modernist concrete fronts, one board-marked tile per cast panel | [Concrete Layers 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/concrete_layers_02) | Rob Tuytel | 2 m |

The game fetches them a moment after the first frame and puts them into one texture array, so the city draws at once without them and stays as it was if they fail to load. Low quality never fetches them, and `#detail=0` turns them off for a side-by-side. To rebuild them (to swap a texture, change its row in the table at the top of the script):

```bash
node scripts/pack-detail.mjs                       # downloads from Poly Haven into a cache folder
node scripts/pack-detail.mjs --src path/to/folder  # or use copies you already have, as <id>/<id>_nor_gl_1k.png, _rough_1k.jpg, _ao_1k.jpg
```

The script needs `ffmpeg` with libwebp. `tests/detail.test.ts` fails if the files grow past 1.5 MB, or if a texture in the script is missing from this page.

## The rotating hallway's corridor: made here, in Blender

Not an asset from anywhere else, but listed because it is not made when the game runs. The hotel corridor (its wainscot and mouldings, the panelled doors and their numbers, the sconces, the ceiling lights, the carpet and the wallpaper) is modelled from numbers by [`art/hallway/build_hallway.py`](art/hallway/build_hallway.py), lit and baked in Blender's Cycles into one lightmap, and compressed with [glTF-Transform](https://gltf-transform.dev) (meshopt for the geometry, WebP for the light). Nothing is downloaded: no models, no textures, no HDRIs.

It is in the repository as [`src/world/hallway/hallway.glb`](src/world/hallway) (about 110 kB) with its `manifest.ts`, both generated by [`scripts/pack-hallway.mjs`](scripts/pack-hallway.mjs), and the Blender scene it was baked from is [`art/hallway/hallway.blend`](art/hallway/hallway.blend), to open and look at. Change the script, not the .blend: the script is the source.

```bash
node scripts/pack-hallway.mjs --blender path/to/blender     # Blender 5.3
node scripts/pack-hallway.mjs --python path/to/python       # or a Python with pip install bpy==5.3.0
node scripts/pack-hallway.mjs --size 512 --samples 32 ...   # a quick draft
```

The shipped bake is the default, 1024 x 1024 at 512 samples (about seven minutes on four CPU cores); the corridor is mirrored from a quarter, so that is as sharp as 2048 x 2048 over the whole of it. `tests/hallwayModel.test.ts` fails if the file grows past 2.5 MB, if anything stands more than 8 cm into the corridor's 7 m box (its walls are walked on), or if the script and the game disagree about its kinds of surface.
